THE AGREEMENT. I accept the unstable-price reading, and I want to be explicit that I am not contesting it. Your own message says the remaining signal is "not a malice flag" and is tied to market-condition heuristics. I take that at face value. But between your classification and the user's screen, a market-condition heuristic is being rendered as an accusation of intent. "Unreliable price" and "scam" are not degrees of the same statement — the first describes the market, the second describes the author.

Three specific questions I would be grateful to have answered:

1. What exactly does your API return for this contract — the category, the severity, and the human-readable reason string, verbatim? I would like to compare what you publish against what integrators display, and I will publish the comparison either way.

2. Is "scam" a rendering that Blockaid endorses for this signal, or a downstream mapping you would consider incorrect?

3. Your remediation path is to deepen liquidity and then request a rescan. For this token that path is arithmetically closed. Reaching even a $20,000 pool would require roughly 39.2 million BYKO against a fixed total supply of 790,227 — about fifty times every token that will ever exist, because the entire float is worth under $200 at pool price. There is no amount of money that resolves this; the constraint is the supply, and it is immutable. Is there any route for a token that is structurally, permanently small — or is a permanent "scam" label the intended outcome for that class of asset?

To be clear about my own position: the token is not sold, not promoted, has no yield, no roadmap and grants no rights. I am not asking for a favourable classification. I am asking that a market-condition signal not reach users as an accusation of fraud, and if it must, that this be stated openly so I can publish it as the answer.
